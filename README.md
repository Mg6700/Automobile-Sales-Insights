## Project Overview

This project analyzes automobile and vehicle product sales data from 2003 to 2005 covering ₹9.60M in revenue across 7 product categories — Classic Cars, Vintage Cars, Motorcycles, Trucks & Buses, Planes, Ships, and Trains. The dashboard tracks revenue, profit, quantity sold, and profit distribution across categories with monthly and annual filtering.

## Key Findings

**1. Classic Cars dominate every metric — revenue, profit, quantity, and margin**
Classic Cars are the undisputed category leader across all four performance dimensions. Any business strategy should prioritize Classic Car inventory and marketing above all other categories.

**2. Overall profit margin is 40% — strong for a vehicle products business**
A 40% profit margin indicates healthy pricing power across the product mix, likely driven by the collectible/premium nature of Classic and Vintage car products.

**3. Revenue peaked sharply in January 2004 (₹0.99M) then normalized**
The revenue trend shows a dramatic January 2004 spike followed by a return to ₹0.28–0.32M range — suggesting a major order or promotional event drove the anomaly rather than sustained demand.

**4. Trains are significantly underperforming — 1.71% profit share, ₹0.19M revenue**
Trains generate the lowest performance across every metric. At 4.3% of quantity and 1.71% of profit, the category may not justify its inventory and operational overhead.

**5. Vintage Cars (19.27% profit share) are a strong secondary category**
While Classic Cars lead, Vintage Cars deliver a meaningful 19.27% of profits at healthy volumes — positioning them as a reliable secondary revenue driver worth continued investment.

**6. A second revenue peak appears in March 2005 (₹0.99M)**
The recurring March spike suggests seasonal demand patterns — possibly trade show season or fiscal year-end purchasing by collectors driving concentrated order activity.
